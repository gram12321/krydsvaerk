# Application Flow

## State owners

- `src/crossword/` owns dictionary entries, layouts and the constraint solver independently of React. A worker checks suggestions away from the interface thread.
- `src/App.tsx` owns the selected template and slot, entered letters and display state. Custom dictionary entries and built-in collection selections are stored in the user's Supabase persistence record.

## Designer interactions and rules

- The designer can start with an empty 9 × 9, 11 × 11 or 13 × 13 grid and construct it with the editing tools. Suggestions, checking and autofill consider words with a clue or image cue, even while other cells and runs are unfinished. Preview and publication still require a complete, valid layout.
- In an unfinished crossing, adjacent occupied letters must fit together in at least one enabled autofill dictionary word that can fit in the available space. Its final start and length are not yet fixed. This is a feasibility check for each unfinished run, not a chosen clue or a promise that the completed grid will be solvable.
- A new template starts empty. Select a slot via its arrow or a letter cell. The shared direction control chooses which word a crossing selects for both writing and autofill; a cell with only one word selects that word. Changing direction also selects the corresponding word at the most recently selected cell, when present. Enter letters directly or insert a suggestion.
- Each displayed suggestion comes from dictionaries enabled for suggestions, matches all entered letters, and is checked for a complete solution using dictionaries enabled for autofill and distinct words. A bounded search may return an uncertain result; those candidates are counted but not presented as confirmed. At most 40 confirmed suggestions are displayed.
- Word autofill checks answers by score and alphabetically within equal scores. Grid autofill searches for the highest total score. Both retain entered letters and show search progress. If the limit is reached, a confirmed best-found answer or grid may be applied with a notice that optimality was not proven; without a confirmed solution, the grid is unchanged. Found-solution counts do not represent all possible solutions because the optimizer prunes branches.
- Changing letters, selection, template or dictionary cancels the previous calculation and starts a new one. Clearing a selected word also clears its crossing letters.
- Named custom dictionaries store word and clue pairs in Supabase. Each dictionary can independently participate in designer suggestions and autofill; existing custom entries are retained in a default dictionary. The player adds entries to a selected dictionary and uses autofill-enabled dictionaries when generating a puzzle. Player progress is stored in Supabase.
- In the dictionary manager, users can publish or unpublish their own custom dictionaries and browse the public shared-dictionary catalog. Adding another user's dictionary saves a read-only copy in the user's dictionary list. Imported dictionaries are disabled for suggestions and autofill by default; users can enable either use independently. Editing a shared dictionary as its owner updates the published copy.
- Users can include or omit the hand-written starter list, short clues, hyphenated words and DanNet. These selections apply to suggestions, autofill and newly generated puzzles, and are saved in Supabase. All built-in entries remain available for validating and publishing manually filled designer answers.
- Designer templates are selected from balanced, long-run and staggered seed families for 9 × 9, 11 × 11 and 13 × 13 grids. Generation varies long slots, 2 × 1 and 1 × 2 clue areas, and black cells; the designer can request a rectangular image area and prefer a separator slot when a matching hyphenated or multiword answer exists in the selected autofill dictionaries. The new-grid action checks multiple candidates with the complete-fill solver and accepts only a candidate with a confirmed full solution; bounded inconclusive candidates are rejected. Search runs in a worker, reports progress, and preserves the current grid if no candidate is confirmed. Player puzzle generation retains its separate curated template path. The designer can check whether a complete fill exists without inserting answers; the check uses a bounded search and reports an inconclusive result if its limit is reached. A toolbar selects letter entry or tools for replacing cells with letters, dark blocks, directional word separators, or 1 × 1, 2 × 1 and 1 × 2 clue areas. Slots and shared clues are derived from the edited grid, with arrows placed on the outward sides of shared clues. A temporarily invalid grid highlights affected cells, explains structural problems and cannot be searched or published; changing a slot removes its custom clue and replacing a letter removes that letter. Clues derived from DanNet are not individually reviewed.
- Designers select one image-area tool and drag across grid cells to mark a rectangle of any size. As with a dark block, adjacent unused clues and isolated unused letters are repaired. Words do not need to cross the rest of the grid, but each word needs a valid cue. Designers can fill an image area with one of three bundled illustrations or a local upload, then drag within it to adjust the crop. Images are unused as cues by default. Designers click the marker next to a row or column on the image to assign it to the nearest answer; each active assignment displays a directional arrow for players to select. The selected image, crop position and arrow links are included in preview and the locally published puzzle.
- Directional separators represent spaces or hyphens within an answer; built-in and custom dictionary entries may contain either. Suggestions and autofill accept both separators. Solving and player navigation skip the separator while retaining its location in the published puzzle.

- The designer can publish a complete grid with distinct dictionary words and optional per-slot clues. Published puzzles are stored as separate copies in a publicly readable Supabase catalog and can be selected by every visitor on the player page. Answers are saved per puzzle, and the active generated or published puzzle is restored. Named unfinished drafts are stored in Supabase and can be reopened in the designer, including invalid layouts. Existing browser storage is imported once for each anonymous Supabase user.
- The restored player page can also generate a random puzzle. Checking and revealing compare against the selected puzzle solution.
